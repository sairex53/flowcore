# Application-intent packet scheduling on Linux

The research question is whether a cooperating sender can move an urgent datagram ahead of its own already queued routine traffic while preserving capacity for independent background flows. The measurable failure is head-of-line queueing inside a single flow at the local sender. This is a scheduling-policy gap in a per-flow-only baseline. Whether it warrants a new kernel interface depends on comparison with stock schedulers that already accept per-packet classification.

## Existing mechanisms

| Mechanism | Applicable behavior | Boundary |
|---|---|---|
| fq_codel | Hashes packets into flow queues and combines deficit scheduling with CoDel. | A trusted packet's request deadline does not independently promote it within its flow. One leaf can serve each authorized class in a hierarchy. [Kernel implementation](https://github.com/torvalds/linux/blob/master/net/sched/sch_fq_codel.c) |
| CAKE | Integrates shaping, flow isolation, AQM and optional DiffServ tins. EF can select a low-latency tin even when the five-tuple stays the same. | Tin policy is aggregate and depends on a trusted marking domain. Socket backpressure can occur before CAKE sees a packet. [Kernel implementation](https://github.com/torvalds/linux/blob/master/net/sched/sch_cake.c) · [iproute2 CAKE manual](https://github.com/iproute2/iproute2/blob/main/man/man8/tc-cake.8) |
| HTB | Enforces local token-bucket class rates and ceilings, and prioritizes borrowing. | Reservations and ceilings are configuration bounds at the shaped interface. They do not guarantee end-to-end throughput or bound every packet's delay. [Kernel implementation](https://github.com/torvalds/linux/blob/master/net/sched/sch_htb.c) |
| ETF | Consumes application-selected transmit times, schedules a local dequeue window and can report expired timestamps via the error queue. | A transmit time and a request completion deadline are different inputs. ETF requires compatible clock and socket settings. [Kernel implementation](https://github.com/torvalds/linux/blob/master/net/sched/sch_etf.c) · [iproute2 ETF manual](https://github.com/iproute2/iproute2/blob/main/man/man8/tc-etf.8) |
| TAPRIO | Opens traffic-class gates according to a configured time schedule. | A gate list represents a periodic schedule. NIC clock and offload capabilities determine which hardware modes can be used. [Kernel implementation](https://github.com/torvalds/linux/blob/master/net/sched/sch_taprio.c) · [iproute2 TAPRIO manual](https://github.com/iproute2/iproute2/blob/main/man/man8/tc-taprio.8) |
| Socket priorities and pacing | Cooperative UDP can set per-message priority. Socket pacing caps and earliest-departure timestamps can shape offered traffic with a compatible scheduler such as FQ. | A priority is a classifier input, and a pacing cap is an upper rate. Neither is proof of identity or of application latency. [Socket options](https://github.com/torvalds/linux/blob/master/net/core/sock.c) · [FQ implementation](https://github.com/torvalds/linux/blob/master/net/sched/sch_fq.c) |
| TC/TCX, cgroup and BPF qdisc | TC/TCX can act on packet metadata. Cgroup programs can help bind operator policy to a workload. A supported BPF qdisc can provide enqueue and dequeue operations through the separate Qdisc_ops struct_ops interface. | Classifier hooks alone have no dequeue control. A verifier-accepted qdisc still needs bounded lifetime, queue ownership, watchdog and deletion tests. The installed kernel has CONFIG_NET_SCH_BPF and BTF qdisc symbols. Load and attach of a custom qdisc were outside this experiment. [BPF attach types](https://docs.kernel.org/bpf/libbpf/program_types.html) · [Kernel BPF qdisc implementation](https://github.com/torvalds/linux/blob/master/net/sched/bpf_qdisc.c) |
| MPTCP | Selects eligible subflows for sends and reinjection. The installed VM reported default as both the active and available scheduler. | Subflow selection is separate from the local qdisc and does not provide arbitrary packet deadlines or request reordering. [MPTCP scheduler](https://github.com/torvalds/linux/blob/master/net/mptcp/sched.c) |

Earlier stock-kernel tests found that later TCP bytes could arrive first on the wire and still wait behind an earlier sequence gap at the receiving application. Transport changes or separate delivery domains address that case. A local packet dequeue policy cannot bypass TCP's ordered delivery contract. [FLOWCORE transport experiments](KERNEL_EXPERIMENT_RESULTS.md) · [TCP specification](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc9293.html)

## Model and choice

- A trusted application supplies a local monotonic deadline and its own datagram boundaries. The lab uses one UDP socket for short requests and routine messages, with a separate sender socket for each background flow.
- The sender reduces admitted slack to a two-class hint. An operator-owned socket mark authenticates admission to the protected class. A small ordinary socket priority expresses the sender's choice per datagram. DSCP EF is emitted on the same datagrams for the CAKE DiffServ control.
- Estimated demand, observed queue delay and available capacity are distinct signals. The study uses frozen offered demand, measured transmit spans and a read-back local rate. The only runtime rate transition is the scripted capacity-collapse case. There is no autonomous backlog-to-priority feedback.
- Per-flow fair scheduling is the baseline. Static hierarchy isolates an entire admitted socket, while urgency-aware hierarchy isolates only selected datagrams. CAKE DiffServ provides another stock urgency implementation. Exact earliest-deadline-first ordering, per-tenant fair virtual finish times and queue-aware urgency are research candidates. Their value would need measurement against both stock urgency variants.
- After the optional application submission check, fq_codel controls queue pressure and drops packets according to its own AQM and limits. The prototype does not track a deadline per queued skb or reorder packets according to arbitrary absolute deadlines. Use ETF for a separate experiment when packet transmit-time expiry is the requirement.

## Safety

The fixture uses an isolated veth pair. Only the root lab runner creates the topology and marked sender. A capability-free client can choose ordinary priority 6, but the HTB classifier also requires the trusted mark. A client with the authority to set network marks or arbitrary HTB class IDs is outside this isolation boundary. The CAKE DiffServ comparison assumes operator-controlled marking. A shared deployment would need a verified DSCP trust boundary.

The prototype fixes two HTB leaves with up to 256 packets and 1 MiB of qdisc memory per fq_codel leaf, plus separate bounded socket buffers. The urgent class has a 30% reservation and a 60% ceiling, while background is reserved 70% and may borrow up to the parent rate. Borrowing and local service depend on queued demand and packet granularity. A nonzero reservation limits starvation at the owned bottleneck. The experiment separately tests empty 250-ms background delivery windows.

One per-message slack check and at most two ematch comparisons add bounded work independent of workload size. The scheduler work remains in Linux HTB and fq_codel with their ordinary scheduling and AQM behavior. The fixture makes no constant-time claim for every kernel drop path. No new qdisc, kernel ABI, TCP logic or BPF program is required for this design.
